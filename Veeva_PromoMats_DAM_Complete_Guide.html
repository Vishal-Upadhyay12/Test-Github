SECTION 1: INTRODUCTION TO DAM AND PROMOMATS

1. Introduction to DAM and Veeva Vault PromoMats

1.1 What is Digital Asset Management (DAM)?

Digital Asset Management (DAM) is a system, process, and strategy for storing, organising, finding, retrieving, and sharing digital assets — such as promotional materials, images, videos, documents, templates, and approved content — in a centralised, controlled, and auditable way.

Simple Analogy: Think of DAM as a super-organised, searchable, permission-controlled library for all your digital content. Instead of hunting through email attachments or shared drives, every approved asset lives in one place, with a full history of who changed what and when.

What qualifies as a Digital Asset?
  • Promotional brochures, detail aids, leave-behinds
  • Digital banners, social media creatives, email templates
  • Sales presentation decks and product one-pagers
  • Regulatory-approved labeling content and core visual aids
  • Training videos, webinar recordings
  • Photography, brand images, logos, icons
  • Reusable content fragments (modular content blocks)

SECTION 2: DAM ARCHITECTURE IN VEEVA VAULT

2. DAM Architecture in Veeva Vault PromoMats

2.1 Architectural Overview

PromoMats DAM architecture consists of four interlocking layers: the Data Layer (objects and metadata), the Content Layer (documents and renditions), the Process Layer (lifecycles, workflows, reviews), and the Integration Layer (CRM, portals, external systems).

PromoMats DAM — Four Architectural Layers
  • Layer 1 — DATA LAYER: Vault Objects (Product, Country, Material Type, Campaign) that categorise and describe assets
  • Layer 2 — CONTENT LAYER: Vault Documents (the actual files) + Renditions (PDF, thumbnail, web-optimised versions)
  • Layer 3 — PROCESS LAYER: Document Lifecycles (Draft → Approved → Expired), MLR workflows, electronic signatures
  • Layer 4 — INTEGRATION LAYER: Veeva CRM connector, Approved Email, CLM player, external portal APIs

2.3 Document vs. Object Architecture in DAM

Documents (the Assets themselves)
  • Physical files: PowerPoint, InDesign, JPEG, MP4, HTML5, PDF
  • Each has: versions (major/minor), a lifecycle state, metadata fields, renditions, and an audit trail
  • Example: A 32-slide detail aid PDF for a drug is a Document in Vault
  • Documents can be referenced by Objects (e.g., a Campaign Object can link to 50 documents)
Objects (the Metadata & Structure)
  • Structured records that describe, categorise, and link documents
  • Examples: Product Object, Campaign Object, Country Object, Content Module Object
  • Objects form a relationship web that makes content findable and reusable
  • Example: All detail aids for Product X in Country Y can be found via Product + Country Object lookups

3.2 PromoMats-Specific Concepts

3.2.1 Modular Content

What it is: A methodology where promotional content is built from pre-approved, reusable modules (blocks of text, images, claims) rather than creating each piece from scratch.

Why it matters: Each module is individually approved through MLR. When you assemble a new piece using only pre-approved modules, the review cycle is dramatically shortened — you only review the combination, not each claim again.

Modular Content in Action
A pharma company has 10 approved text modules and 5 approved image modules for Drug X. A new country wants a detail aid. Instead of writing new content and going through a full 8-week MLR, they select relevant pre-approved modules, assemble the detail aid, and submit only for a short review of layout/combination — saving 6 weeks of review time.

3.2.2 Claims Management

In pharma, every promotional statement (claim) must be substantiated by clinical evidence. PromoMats manages this through:

  • Claim Library: a repository of all pre-approved promotional claims
  • Claim Linking: each claim in a document is linked to its supporting evidence document
  • Claim Approval: claims go through their own approval process before being usable in materials
  • Claim Traceability: auditors can trace any statement back to the clinical study that supports it
Claims Management Scenario
A medical writer states "Drug X reduces HbA1c by 1.2% vs placebo" in a detail aid. In PromoMats, this statement is linked to the Phase 3 trial publication stored in Vault. The Medical reviewer can instantly verify the claim is accurate. If the study is later updated, PromoMats flags all documents using that claim for re-review.

3.2.3 Country/Market Management

Global pharma companies have one global asset that must be adapted for 50+ countries. PromoMats manages this through:

  • Global-to-Local workflow: Global creates master → Local adapts for language, regulations, approved indications
  • Country Object: each country has its own Object record defining regulatory requirements
  • Country-specific Lifecycles: some markets have stricter review processes
  • Indication mapping: a product may have different approved indications per country
SECTION 4: KEY OBJECTS IN PROMOMATS DAM

4. Key Objects in PromoMats DAM

PromoMats uses Vault Objects as the metadata and structural backbone of the DAM. Here are the most important objects and their roles:

4.1 Product Object (product__v)

Product Object

Represents a pharmaceutical product or brand being promoted.

Key Fields: product_name__v (Text), therapeutic_area__v (Picklist), active_ingredient__c (Text), brand_name__c (Text), product_status__v (Picklist: Active/Discontinued), launch_date__c (Date)

Relationship to DAM: Every promotional document must be linked to at least one Product record. Enables filtering: "Show me all approved detail aids for Lipitor 40mg"

4.2 Country Object (country__v)

Country Object

Represents a market/country where promotional content is used.

Key Fields: country_name__v (Text), iso_code__c (Text), regulatory_body__c (Text), language__c (Picklist), review_requirements__c (Long Text)

Relationship to DAM: Documents are tagged to countries, enabling country-filtered distribution. Use: "Show all Approved assets for Germany that are currently active"

4.3 Campaign Object

Campaign Object

Groups multiple documents/assets that belong to the same promotional campaign.

Key Fields: campaign_name__c (Text), campaign_start_date__c (Date), campaign_end_date__c (Date), related_product__c (Lookup to Product), campaign_status__c (Picklist: Planning/Active/Closed), target_audience__c (Picklist)

Use: All 15 assets created for a Spring 2024 cardiovascular campaign are linked to this Campaign record. When the campaign ends, all linked assets can be batch-expired together.

4.4 Claim Object

Claim Object

A library of pre-approved promotional statements.

Key Fields: claim_text__c (Long Text), claim_category__c (Picklist: Efficacy/Safety/Dosing/Pharmacology), approval_status__c (Picklist), supporting_evidence__c (Document Lookup), approved_markets__c (Multi-select), expiry_date__c (Date)

Key Relationship: Claim records are linked to Document records that USE the claim. Creates full traceability from finished material back to clinical evidence.

4.5 Content Module Object

Content Module Object

A reusable, MLR-approved block of content (text, image, or both).

Key Fields: module_name__c (Text), module_type__c (Picklist: Text Only/Image Only/Text+Image), module_content__c (Long Text), related_claims__c (Lookup to Claim), applicable_products__c (Lookup), applicable_countries__c (Lookup), module_status__c (Picklist)

Use Case: When assembling a detail aid using modular content, each module is pre-approved. The assembled document goes through a shorter "combination review" rather than full MLR.

SECTION 6: END-TO-END PROMOMATS PROCESS

6. End-to-End PromoMats Process

6.1 Stage 1: Content Brief & Initiation

  1. Marketing/Brand team identifies a content need (e.g., new detail aid for upcoming drug launch)
  2. A Content Brief is created — often as a Vault document or via a Job Bag request form
  3. The Coordinator creates the document record in PromoMats with initial metadata (Product, Country, Material Type, Campaign)
  4. The document enters the DRAFT lifecycle state
  5. The Author (medical writer, agency) is assigned as owner and begins creating the content
What happens in Vault at this stage
  • Document record created with status = Draft
  • Metadata fields completed: Product, Country, Type, Audience, Owner
  • Version 0.1 created (draft major version)
  • Document is visible only to the Author/Owner and Coordinator at this point

6.2 Stage 2: Content Creation & Claims Linking

  1. Author creates the promotional content (in agency tools like Adobe InDesign, PowerPoint, etc.)
  2. Content is uploaded to Vault as the source document
  3. Vault auto-generates a Viewable Rendition (PDF) for review
  4. Author links Claims to the document — every significant statement is linked to a pre-approved Claim record
  5. Supporting evidence documents (clinical publications, PI) are linked as Document References
  6. If using Modular Content: Author selects pre-approved modules instead of writing from scratch
Claim Linking in practice
  • The author highlights "reduces HbA1c by 1.2%" in the document
  • Links it to Claim #CLM-0042 (pre-approved for US and EU)
  • Claim #CLM-0042 is already linked to the Phase 3 study publication in Vault
  • The evidence chain is now complete and traceable

6.3 Stage 3: Internal Review & Coordination (MLR)

  1. Author submits document for internal review ("Submit for Review" lifecycle action)
  2. Document moves to "In Review" state
  3. Coordinator assigns reviewers: Medical, Legal, Regulatory (and optionally Brand/Marketing)
  4. Each reviewer receives an in-app notification and task
  5. Reviewers annotate directly on the Viewable Rendition in Vault
  6. All annotations are captured in the audit trail
  7. Author responds to comments, makes edits, uploads a new minor version (0.2, 0.3...)
MLR Review cycle detail
  • Medical Reviewer: checks scientific accuracy, claim substantiation
  • Legal Reviewer: checks fair balance, IP, competitive statements
  • Regulatory Reviewer: checks compliance with approved label, local regulations
  • Parallel or Sequential review — configurable per Vault workflow setup
  • Each reviewer clicks "Approve" or "Reject with Comments"
  • If ANY reviewer rejects — document goes back to Author for revision

6.4 Stage 4: Approval & Final Sign-Off

  1. Once all reviewers have approved, the Coordinator performs a final check
  2. Electronic signature is collected (21 CFR Part 11 compliant e-signature in Vault)
  3. Document transitions to "Approved" state (major version promoted: 0.3 → 1.0)
  4. Vault locks the Approved Rendition — it cannot be modified
  5. Expiry date is set (typically 2 years from approval for promotional materials)
  6. Document becomes visible and distributable to authorised users
What changes at Approved state
  • Document version becomes 1.0 (major version)
  • Approved Rendition is generated and locked
  • Source document access restricted (based on permissions)
  • Content becomes visible in Veeva CRM (if configured)
  • Expiry date activates — countdown begins
  • All review annotations archived in audit trail

6.5 Stage 5: Distribution & Delivery

Distribution ChannelHow PromoMats Delivers
Veeva CRM (Field Reps)Approved CLM content automatically pushed to CRM via Vault Connect. Field reps see new content on iPads within hours of approval
Approved EmailApproved email templates appear in the rep's CRM email composer. Rep cannot alter the approved text — they can only personalise the salutation
Internal Portal / IntranetDocuments available for download by internal teams via the Vault interface or embedded portal
External Agency PortalAgencies can access Vault (with restricted permissions) to download approved templates for adaptation

Regulatory SubmissionPromotional materials submitted to regulatory bodies (FDA OPDP in the US) directly from Vault

6.6 Stage 6: Maintenance, Expiry & Withdrawal

  1. The system monitors expiry dates and sends notifications 60/30/7 days before expiry
  2. Content owners review: extend, update, or let expire
  3. If an update is needed: document enters a new draft cycle with version increment (2.0 in progress)
  4. On expiry date: document automatically transitions to "Expired" state — pulled from all distribution channels
  5. If urgent withdrawal needed (safety signal): Coordinator manually triggers "Withdraw" action
  6. Withdrawn documents are immediately recalled from Veeva CRM and all channels
  7. All withdrawal actions are logged with timestamp and user in the audit trail

9.2 Configuration Best Practices

  • Start with standard: Use PromoMats standard configuration as much as possible before customising. Every custom object/field adds maintenance burden.
  • Metadata before documents: Build out your Object taxonomy completely before loading any documents. Retro-fitting metadata is painful.
  • Lifecycle first: Design and test your lifecycle in a sandbox with real stakeholders walking through it manually before going live.
  • Security by role: Define your security model on paper before configuring. It is much harder to adjust security after users are live.
  • Picklist governance: Establish a picklist governance process from day one. Uncontrolled picklists explode into chaos quickly.
  • Document naming conventions: Enforce a naming standard via validation rules.
  • Test with real content: UAT must use actual promotional documents, not dummy files.
  • Train before go-live: Role-specific training is critical. A Medical Reviewer needs different training from a Marketing Author.
SECTION 10: IMPLEMENTATION ISSUES & HOW TO RESOLVE

10. Implementation Issues & How to Resolve Them

10.1 Metadata & Taxonomy Issues

[Design] Incomplete metadata model — required fields not identified during requirements
Impact: Documents uploaded without key metadata. Assets unfindable. MLR process cannot enforce completeness.
Resolution: Go back to requirements. Add missing fields. Use validation rules to enforce mandatory metadata before documents can be submitted for review. Run a retroactive data cleanup exercise.
[Design] Uncontrolled picklist values — no governance for who adds values
Impact: Duplicate values proliferate ("Detail Aid", "DetailAid", "Detail aid" are all different). Reports inconsistent. Filtering breaks.
Resolution: Establish a Picklist Governance Board. Use a Change Request process for adding/modifying values. Consolidate duplicates via data cleanup. Add descriptions to each picklist value.
[Build] Document type hierarchy too deep or too flat
Impact: Too deep: Users cannot find the right type. Too flat: No meaningful categorisation. Both lead to mis-filed documents.
Resolution: Best practice: 2–3 levels of hierarchy. Run a card-sort exercise with representative end users to validate the taxonomy before building.

10.2 Lifecycle & Workflow Issues

[Build] Lifecycle is too complex with too many states and transitions
Impact: Users do not understand the process. Documents get "stuck" in states. Adoption fails.
Resolution: Simplify first. Start with 5–6 states maximum. Draw the lifecycle as a flowchart and walk every stakeholder through it before building.
[Build] Parallel vs Sequential review not correctly configured
Impact: All three reviewers configured as sequential when stakeholders expected parallel, tripling review time.
Resolution: During requirements, explicitly ask: "Do all three reviewers review at the same time, or does Legal review only after Medical approves?" Document the decision in writing with sign-off from all stakeholders.
[UAT] Entry criteria too strict — users cannot progress documents even when they should
Impact: Documents stuck in Draft. Blocks testing progress.
Resolution: Test every lifecycle transition independently with a test record. Create a test script that explicitly tests each combination of field values against each transition. Fix formula errors before UAT begins.

10.3 Integration Issues

[Build] Vault-to-CRM connector sync failures — content not appearing on rep iPads
Impact: Sales reps cannot access newly approved CLM content. Commercial launch delayed.
Resolution: Verify: (1) Document type is in the CRM-distributable list, (2) Document is in a distributive lifecycle state, (3) Product field value matches a CRM product record exactly (case-sensitive), (4) Rep user has the correct CRM profile. Check Vault sync logs for error messages.
[Migration] Legacy content migration — documents loaded without correct metadata
Impact: Thousands of migrated documents have blank Product or Country fields. System appears broken.
Resolution: Pre-migration: run metadata enrichment on source content before loading. Use Vault Loader upsert to add metadata post-load. Never migrate without a field mapping spreadsheet validated by business owners.
[Build] SSO integration failure — users cannot log in after go-live
Impact: All users locked out. Business operations halted.
Resolution: Always maintain at least 2 Vault native user accounts (not SSO) as emergency break-glass accounts. Test SSO thoroughly in UAT with real production IdP settings. Have a rollback plan for the first 48 hours post-go-live.

10.4 Security & Access Issues

[Build] Over-permissioned roles — users can see content they should not
Impact: Country A users can see unapproved drafts intended for Country B. Compliance risk.
Resolution: Implement country-based sharing rules from day one. Design security with "least privilege" principle. Use Dynamic Access Control to restrict access based on metadata values.
[Build] Under-permissioned roles — users blocked from necessary actions
Impact: Medical Reviewers cannot see the Viewable Rendition to annotate. Review process halted.
Resolution: Walk through every user role's daily tasks and verify each action is permitted. Use a Security Configuration Matrix spreadsheet mapping every role to every permission before building.

[Post Go-Live] Search returning too many irrelevant results
Impact: Users cannot find relevant content quickly. Perceived as DAM failure.
Resolution: (1) Review and improve metadata quality (richer metadata = better search). (2) Train users on advanced search filters (Product + Country + Status combination). (3) Create saved search templates. (4) Create curated content collections for key user groups.

SECTION 14: QnA & SCENARIOS

14. QnA & Scenarios

What is the difference between DAM and a Document Management System (DMS)?
A Document Management System focuses on version control, audit trails, and lifecycle for official documents (SOPs, regulatory submissions). A DAM focuses on organising, finding, distributing, and reusing digital promotional assets. PromoMats combines both: it has DMS-grade compliance and audit trail plus DAM capabilities for promotional content discovery, reuse, and distribution to commercial channels.
Why would you put a document in "Withdrawn" vs letting it "Expire"?
Expiry is a planned, scheduled event — the document has reached its validity end date and is retired normally. Withdrawal is an emergency, unplanned recall triggered by a specific problem (safety signal discovered, incorrect claim found, regulatory feedback). Withdrawal is immediate and must be accompanied by a documented reason. Expiry is automatic; withdrawal is manual and requires authorisation.
A rep calls saying they cannot see the new detail aid on their iPad. How do you troubleshoot this?
1. Verify the document is in "Approved for Distribution" state in Vault. 2. Check the document type — is it in the list of CRM-distributable types? 3. Check the Product field matches exactly the CRM product name (case-sensitive). 4. Check the Vault Connect sync log for error messages. 5. Check the rep user has the correct CRM profile with access to this product. 6. Verify the sync has run since the approval. 7. Check if the rep's iPad is connected and has synced.
What is modular content and why does it matter for review cycle time?
Modular content is the practice of building promotional materials from pre-approved, reusable content blocks (modules). Each module is individually approved through full MLR review. When assembling a new piece from pre-approved modules, only the layout/combination is reviewed — not the content of each module again. A traditional full MLR review takes 6–10 weeks. A modular content combination review can take 1–2 weeks, dramatically accelerating time-to-market.
What are the regulatory implications of expiry date management in PromoMats?
FDA OPDP requires that promotional materials reflect the current approved label. If a PI (Prescribing Information) is updated, materials based on the old PI must be recalled or updated. PromoMats manages this through expiry dates and document references — when the PI is updated, all documents referencing it can be flagged for review. Appropriate expiry dates (typically 2 years max) and a renewal process ensure the company never distributes content that contradicts the current approved label — which carries significant regulatory risk.
Claim Change Cascade
A pivotal clinical study is updated, changing the endpoint from 1.2% to 1.1% HbA1c reduction. In PromoMats: (1) The updated study publication is uploaded as a new version. (2) The Claim record CLM-0042 is updated with the new figure and goes back through approval. (3) Once the updated claim is approved, PromoMats generates a report of all 47 documents across 12 countries that use CLM-0042. (4) Each document owner is notified and a new review cycle begins. Without PromoMats, this impact analysis would take weeks and likely miss some materials.
Global-to-Local Localisation
The global Medical Affairs team creates a new detail aid for Drug Y targeting cardiologists. The global document is approved in PromoMats. The French affiliate needs a French version compliant with ANSM (French regulator) requirements. Process: (1) French team creates a child document linked to the global parent. (2) Sent to a TMS for translation. (3) Translated French content comes back to Vault linked to the original. (4) French version goes through French-specific review. (5) French version approved and distributed to French CRM users only, controlled by the Country field.

15. Interview Questions — 80+ with Answers

Category A: DAM Fundamentals (Q1–Q15)
Q1. What is DAM and how does it differ from a traditional file storage system?
DAM (Digital Asset Management) is a system for organising, storing, and distributing digital assets with rich metadata, controlled access, lifecycle management, and audit trails. Unlike a file storage system (SharePoint, network drive), a DAM enforces metadata, provides advanced search, tracks version history, manages approval workflows, supports rights/expiry management, and integrates with distribution channels. In Veeva PromoMats, DAM is further specialised for regulatory compliance in life sciences.
Q2. Why is Veeva Vault PromoMats preferred over a generic DAM in pharma?
PromoMats is purpose-built for life sciences. It has native MLR (Medical-Legal-Regulatory) review workflows, 21 CFR Part 11 compliant electronic signatures, tamper-proof audit trails, native Veeva CRM integration for field content delivery, built-in claims management and evidence linking, expiry and withdrawal management for regulatory compliance, and modular content capabilities. Generic DAM solutions would require significant customisation to achieve any of these capabilities and would still lack the regulatory depth.
Q3. Explain the concept of a Document Lifecycle in PromoMats.
A Document Lifecycle is the state machine that governs a document's journey from creation to retirement. In PromoMats, typical states are: Draft (creation/editing), In Review (MLR process), Approved for Distribution (ready for field use), Superseded (replaced by newer version), Expired (validity period ended), and Withdrawn (emergency recall). Each transition is controlled by specific rules and permissions — e.g., only a Regulatory Reviewer can transition from "In Review" to "Approved." Every state change is audit-logged with timestamp and user identity.
Q4. What is a rendition in Vault PromoMats and why are multiple renditions needed?
A rendition is an alternative format representation of the same document. Multiple renditions serve different purposes: the Viewable Rendition (PDF) is used for in-browser review and annotation by MLR reviewers; the Thumbnail is used in search results and list views; the Approved Rendition is the final locked version for distribution; the Source Rendition is the original editable file (protected); the CLM Rendition is the HTML5/ZIP package for iPad delivery. This separation ensures reviewers always see a consistent format, and that the source file is protected from the distributed version.
Q5. What is MLR review and what are the three components?
MLR stands for Medical, Legal, and Regulatory review — the three mandatory approval functions for pharmaceutical promotional content. Medical review: verifies scientific accuracy, data integrity, claim substantiation against clinical evidence. Legal review: checks for intellectual property issues, competitive claims, fair balance requirements, legal language. Regulatory review: ensures content complies with the approved product label (PI), meets country-specific regulations, and is appropriate for the approved indication. All three must approve before content can be distributed.
Q6. What is the difference between version 0.1 and 1.0 in PromoMats?
0.1 (and 0.2, 0.3...) are minor versions representing draft iterations during the review process — work in progress. These are not distributable and cannot be pushed to CRM. 1.0 is the first major (approved) version — created when a document successfully completes the MLR review process and reaches the Approved lifecycle state. Major versions (1.0, 2.0, 3.0) have locked Approved Renditions and are distributable. When a document is revised and re-approved, it becomes 2.0, superseding 1.0.
Q7. Explain what Claims Management is in PromoMats.
Claims Management is the process of maintaining a library of pre-approved promotional statements (claims) with full evidence linkage. Each claim is individually approved and linked to the clinical study or data source that substantiates it. When a claim is used in a promotional document, a link is created between that document and the Claim record. This creates full traceability: any auditor can trace a statement in a detail aid back through the Claim record to the clinical publication that proves it. If the underlying evidence changes, all documents using the affected claim are automatically flagged for review.
Q8. What is Approved Email and how does it work end-to-end?
Approved Email (AE) is a Veeva feature that lets pharmaceutical companies create MLR-approved email templates that sales reps send to HCPs via Veeva CRM. End-to-end: (1) Author creates the email template in PromoMats with approved content and subject line. (2) Template goes through MLR review. (3) On approval, Vault Connect pushes the template to Veeva CRM. (4) Rep selects the approved template in CRM, can only customise specific approved fields (typically salutation). (5) Rep sends the email. (6) Engagement data (open, click) flows back to CRM. The rep cannot modify the approved content — ensuring compliance.
Q9. What is the difference between content Expiry and Withdrawal in PromoMats?
Expiry is a planned, time-based event — the document reaches its pre-set validity end date and is automatically transitioned to Expired state. It is expected and managed proactively. Withdrawal is an emergency, manual action taken when approved content must be immediately recalled due to an unexpected problem — a safety signal, a discovered error, regulatory feedback. Withdrawal is immediate and triggered by a Coordinator or Admin with documented justification. Both result in content removal from distribution, but withdrawal is reactive and urgent while expiry is planned.
Q10. What is CLM (Closed Loop Marketing) in the context of PromoMats?
CLM is a commercial model where sales reps present digital content (slides, interactive presentations) to HCPs on iPads during sales calls, using Veeva CRM. The "closed loop" refers to: content approved in PromoMats is pushed to rep iPads → rep presents to HCP and the CRM captures which slides were shown and for how long → this interaction data flows back to Marketing in PromoMats → Marketing uses this data to improve future content. PromoMats manages the CLM content lifecycle and distribution; Veeva CRM handles the presentation and data capture.
Q11. How does PromoMats support Global-to-Local content management?
PromoMats supports global-to-local through: parent-child document relationships (global master → local adaptation), country-specific lifecycles (different review requirements per market), dynamic access control based on Country metadata (French users only see French-approved content), country-specific picklist values and mandatory fields configured per country, and localisation workflow routing that ensures only the relevant country's reviewers receive a local adaptation for review. Global teams publish a master; local affiliates create related child documents that inherit core metadata but have country-specific review and approval.
Q12. What is modular content and why is it important?
Modular content is the practice of building promotional materials from pre-approved, individually reviewed content blocks (modules). Each module — a claim statement, a chart, an efficacy table, an image — is approved once through MLR. When a new piece is assembled from pre-approved modules, only the combination/layout needs review rather than every content element. This reduces review cycle time from 6–10 weeks to 1–2 weeks. It also ensures consistent, approved messaging across all materials and markets.
Q13. What metadata is typically required on every promotional document in PromoMats?
Core mandatory metadata typically includes: Product (which drug), Country/Market (which geography), Document Type/Material Type (what kind of asset), Audience (HCP, Patient, Payer), Language, Owner/Author, Expiry Date. Secondary metadata includes: Campaign, Indication, Target Specialty, Distribution Channel, Creation Date, Approval Date. Metadata is enforced through Required field settings and Validation Rules that prevent submission until all mandatory fields are completed.
Q14. Explain the concept of Document References in PromoMats.
Document References are formal links from a promotional document to other documents that support or relate to it. The most critical use is linking a promotional piece to its supporting clinical evidence. For example, a detail aid claiming "Drug X reduces event risk by 25%" is linked to the published clinical trial that proves this. Regulatory reviewers can instantly navigate to the source evidence. If the source document is updated or withdrawn, all promotional documents referencing it are flagged for re-review.
Q15. What is Dynamic Access Control (DAC) in PromoMats?
Dynamic Access Control is a security mechanism that restricts document visibility based on metadata values rather than (or in addition to) user roles. For example, if a document has Country = Germany, DAC can be configured so only users with Germany in their user profile can see that document — even if they have the same system role as a US user. This is critical in global PromoMats implementations where different affiliates must not see each other's unapproved drafts.
Category B: Object Model & Configuration (Q16–Q35)
Q16. What are the key Vault Objects in a PromoMats DAM implementation?
Core objects include: Product Object (the promoted drug/brand), Country Object (markets), Campaign Object (promotional project grouping), Claim Object (approved promotional statements), Content Module Object (reusable approved content blocks), and Material Type (Document Type classification). Supporting objects include User, Language, Therapeutic Area, and any custom objects built for client-specific needs like Job Bag, Content Brief, Regulatory Submission Record, or Distribution Channel.
Q17. How does the Product Object relate to Documents in PromoMats?
Every promotional document should have a lookup to the Product Object. The document has a Product metadata field (a Lookup field pointing to Product__v). This creates a relationship: navigate to a Product record and see all documents related to that product in a Related Documents section. Conversely, on a document, click the Product lookup to navigate to the Product record. This enables product-based content filtering — "show all approved detail aids for Drug X."
Q18. What is the purpose of the Campaign Object in PromoMats DAM?
The Campaign Object groups all documents and assets created for a specific promotional campaign. It stores: campaign name, dates, product association, target audience, budget, status, and the responsible brand manager. Documents created within a campaign are linked to the Campaign record. This enables: viewing all assets in a campaign, batch operations (expire all campaign assets when campaign ends), campaign-level reporting (how many pieces in review, approved, expired), and cross-functional alignment on campaign timelines.
Q19. Explain how Claims are linked to Documents in PromoMats.
Claims are linked to documents through a Document-Claim relationship. When an author submits a document for review, they annotate specific statements in the document and link each statement to a Claim record from the Claim Library. The relationship is stored as a document relationship object or a cross-reference field. Medical reviewers can see each claim in the document and immediately navigate to the Claim record, verify it is approved, and see the linked clinical evidence. This creates a fully traceable evidence chain.
Q20. How is the Document Type hierarchy configured in PromoMats?
The Document Type hierarchy is configured in Admin > Configuration > Document Types in Vault. It is a tree structure where each level narrows the classification: top level (Promotional Materials) → second level (Sales Tools) → third level (Detail Aid). Each Document Type node can have its own metadata schema (fields), lifecycle assignment, security profile, and rendition settings. Best practice is 2–3 levels of hierarchy to balance specificity with usability.
Q21. What is the difference between document-level metadata and object-level data in PromoMats?
Document-level metadata is information stored directly on the document record itself — fields like Product, Country, Expiry Date, Document Type, Owner. These are properties of the asset itself. Object-level data refers to information stored in related Vault Objects — the Product Object stores product description, therapeutic area, and launch date. The document is linked to these objects via lookup fields, inheriting context from them. Changing the Product Object description automatically changes how the product appears when viewing any linked document.
Q22. How would you configure a PromoMats system to support multiple products with different review requirements?
Use a combination of: (1) Document Types per product type or material type, each with its own lifecycle. (2) Object Types to differentiate behaviour per product. (3) Workflow configuration with product-specific reviewer assignment rules (if Product = Drug X, assign to Medical Reviewer Group X). (4) Document creation profiles per product that pre-fill metadata and assign the correct lifecycle. (5) Security profiles that restrict who can create/review content for each product.
Q23. What validation rules would you implement on PromoMats documents?
Essential validation rules: (1) Expiry Date must be in the future when submitting for review. (2) Expiry Date must not be more than 2 years from today. (3) Product field is required before submitting. (4) Country field is required before submitting. (5) At least one Claim must be linked for Promotional Material document types. (6) Document name must follow the naming convention pattern (enforce via REGEX). (7) Approved Email templates must have a subject line field completed.
Q24. Explain how security is configured for a multi-country PromoMats implementation.
Multi-country security uses several layers: (1) Application Roles define what users can do. (2) Dynamic Access Control restricts which documents users can see based on Country metadata matching the user's assigned country. (3) Security profiles per country-role combination. (4) Document sharing rules for cross-country collaboration (global teams see all; local teams see only their country). (5) Object permissions ensure country teams can create/edit documents for their market but not others.
Q25. What is the role of the Content Module Object in PromoMats?
The Content Module Object is the backbone of a modular content strategy. It stores individual pre-approved content blocks — each with its approved text, associated images, linked claims, applicable products and countries, and its own lifecycle status (Approved/Retired). When building new promotional materials using modular content, authors select from the approved module library instead of writing new content. If a module is retired or updated, all documents using it can be flagged. The module itself has gone through full MLR review; the assembled document only needs a combination/layout review.
Q26. How does PromoMats handle document versioning when a major update is needed after approval?
When an approved document (v1.0) needs updating: (1) The owner initiates a new draft cycle — a new minor version (1.1) is created. (2) The new version goes through the same MLR review process. (3) When approved, it becomes v2.0 — a new major version. (4) Vault automatically transitions the previous approved version (1.0) to "Superseded" state. (5) v2.0 is now the active version in distribution. The CRM is updated to show only v2.0 to field reps.
Q27. What is a Job Bag in PromoMats?
A Job Bag is a project record used to manage and track all components of a single promotional project. It acts as the umbrella for all documents, tasks, reviews, and approvals related to one piece of promotional content from brief to distribution. A Job Bag Object typically contains fields for: project name, product, campaign, material type, target audience, brief description, agency assigned, timeline milestones, status, and links to all the documents created within the project.

(3) Map PromoMats Product fields to CRM Product fields. (4) Configure the CRM Distribution User Profile. (5) Define the sync frequency (near real-time recommended). (6) In CRM Vault, configure receiving settings. (7) Test with a pilot document. (8) Monitor the sync log for errors.

(5) Approved Content Inventory — all currently approved content by product and country. (6) Content Withdrawal Report — all withdrawn documents with reason and impact. (7) CRM Sync Status — content successfully pushed to field reps.
Q30. What is the purpose of the Viewable Rendition in PromoMats?
The Viewable Rendition is an automatically generated PDF version of the uploaded source document. Its purpose: (1) It is the standard format used by all MLR reviewers for annotation — regardless of whether the source file is a PPTX, INDD, or other format. (2) Annotations (reviewer comments) are made directly on it in Vault's annotation tool. (3) It protects the source file — reviewers see and comment on the PDF, not the editable source. (4) It is generated fresh with each new document version. Key distinction: the Viewable Rendition is for review; the Approved Rendition is the locked final version for distribution.
Q31. How would you set up expiry management for promotional documents in PromoMats?
Setup: (1) Add Expiry Date as a required date field on promotional document types. (2) Add a lifecycle entry action that sets expiry date when document is approved (TODAY() + 730 days = 2 years). (3) Configure automated notifications: 90, 60, 30, 7 days before expiry. (4) Create an Expiry Dashboard visible to all content owners. (5) Configure the lifecycle to automatically transition documents to Expired state on the expiry date. (6) Ensure the Vault Connect CRM sync removes expired content immediately on state transition.
Q32. What is the difference between the Approved Rendition and the Source Rendition?
The Source Rendition is the original file uploaded by the author — the editable source (e.g., InDesign file, PowerPoint). It contains all editing layers and is typically restricted to authors and design teams. The Approved Rendition is a locked PDF generated when the document reaches Approved state — it represents exactly what was reviewed and approved and cannot be modified. The Approved Rendition is what is distributed to field reps via CRM and preserved for compliance audit.
Q33. How do you handle the retirement of a Content Module in modular content management?
When a Content Module needs to be retired: (1) Change the Module status to "Under Review" — it can still be used in existing approved documents but cannot be selected for new materials. (2) Run a Module Usage Report to identify all documents that use this module. (3) Notify document owners that their materials will need updating. (4) Update the module with new approved content through its own review cycle. (5) When the updated module is approved, retire the old version. (6) Trigger re-review of all documents using the old module.
Q34. What are the most important fields to include in a PromoMats document type configuration?
Mandatory fields: Product (Lookup to Product Object), Country (Lookup or multi-select picklist), Document Type (part of type hierarchy), Language (Picklist), Audience (Picklist: HCP/Patient/Payer), Expiry Date (Date), Owner (User Lookup). Important optional fields: Campaign (Lookup), Indication (Picklist), Target Specialty (Picklist), Regulatory Reference Number, Global/Local flag, Parent Document (for adaptations), Distribution Channels. System fields (auto-populated): Created Date, Modified Date, Created By, Approved Date, Version.
Q35. How would you configure parallel vs sequential MLR review in PromoMats?
Sequential review: the document routes first to Medical, Medical must approve before Legal receives it, Legal must approve before Regulatory. Total time = sum of all review times. Parallel review: all three reviewers receive the review task simultaneously when the document enters "In Review." Total time = the longest individual review time. Hybrid (most common): Medical and Regulatory review in parallel; Legal joins in a second parallel phase. Configuration: in the Vault Workflow configuration, define participant assignment rules, step sequence (parallel = same step number for all participants), and the "all approve" condition.

(4) Each country version undergoes its own local review (which may be shorter if the global review covered the core claims). (5) Country versions are tagged with their Country metadata and only distributed to CRM users in that country. (6) When the global master is updated, all 15 country versions are automatically flagged for review.

Using VQL or Vault reports: query the audit trail for documents, find the "In Review" entry date and "Approved" entry date, calculate the difference. Q48. What is the difference between a Vault Workflow and a Lifecycle in PromoMats?
In Vault, a Lifecycle is the state machine — it defines the states a document can be in (Draft, In Review, Approved) and the rules for transitioning between them. A Workflow is a structured task-routing process — it defines who does what, in what order, with what deadlines. The Lifecycle controls the document state; the Workflow routes tasks to reviewers during the "In Review" state. They are separate but connected: the Workflow completion triggers the Lifecycle transition from "In Review" to "Approved."

Q79. Describe the relationship between Vault PromoMats, Veeva CRM, and a sales rep's iPad.
The relationship: PromoMats is the authoring, review, and approval system — all content is created, reviewed, and approved here. Vault Connect is the integration layer that monitors PromoMats for newly approved content and syncs it to Veeva CRM. Veeva CRM is the delivery system — it stores the approved content library and makes it available to field reps. The rep's iPad runs the Veeva CRM app — when connected (with a sync), it downloads the latest approved content from CRM.
